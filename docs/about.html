About the NYPD Weekly Archive

NYPD posts its weekly precinct statistics on nyc.gov and replaces them the next week. We save a copy of every file, every week, and publish them here so the numbers stay public.

Built by BetaNYC #

BetaNYC is New York's civic organization dedicated to improving lives through public interest technology, open data, and civic design. We build tools, train the public, and advocate for a more transparent, accountable, and participatory city government. BetaNYC is a project of the Fund for the City of New York.

Others saved these files long before we did, and this archive builds on their work:

  • Aaron Schumacher saved NYPD's CompStat PDFs from 2014 to 2016 (ajschumacher/nypd).
  • Tim McDermott, a BetaNYC volunteer, turned the weekly CompStat reports into CSV files from 2015 to February 2026.
  • Josh Greenman's nypd-compstat-scraper has read NYPD's CompStat spreadsheets since March 2026. We use his numbers with his permission.
  • The Internet Archive's Wayback Machine holds copies of many of NYPD's files from earlier years.

For five of these reports, NYPD's list points to datasets of single records: one row per complaint, arrest, summons or crash. The weekly reports themselves, NYPD's own count for each precinct, development and week, are not on Open Data, and NYPD replaces them on its website every week. This archive keeps each week's copy. Our copies have gaps too; each report's page shows which weeks we hold.

“We did not find” means our search of the NYC Open Data catalog on November 22, 2026 turned up nothing, not that it cannot exist. If you know of a copy, tell us.

How to check a week yourself #

We ran these counts on November 24, 2026, with the command nypd-archive recount in this archive's code. Open Data rows can change after NYPD prints its weekly report, so a count you run later may differ a little from ours. Every number we compared is in data/recount/, one file per report. summary.csv there has one row per report, week and period, with the query we ran.

On November 24, 2026, the crime complaint data reached September 30, 2026, the crash data November 20, 2026, and the 911 data September 30, 2026. The 311 data was current. We did not check a week the data does not fully cover. We also left out weeks that cross New Year's Day, because we do not know whether NYPD's count for such a week starts on January 1.

CompStat

Recount the seven major crimes for one precinct-week from NYPD Complaint Data Current (Year To Date):

  1. Pick a week. NYPD's weeks run Monday to Sunday. The dataset does not reach the present: on November 24, 2026 its newest rows were from September 30, 2026. For 2025 and earlier, use NYPD Complaint Data Historic, which has the same columns.
  2. Keep the rows whose report date (rpt_dt) falls in that week. Use the day the crime was reported, not the day it happened. When we used the day it happened, only a little over half the counts matched.
  3. Keep the seven major crimes, by offense code (ky_cd): 101 murder, 104 rape, 105 robbery, 106 felony assault, 107 burglary, 109 grand larceny, 110 grand larceny of a motor vehicle.
  4. Count the rows for each precinct (addr_pct_cd) and crime.
  5. Compare with our file for that week. Use the rows where section is Crime Complaints, period is wtd and year is the year you are checking. Our file names the crimes NYPD's way: Fel. Assault, Gr. Larceny, and G.L.A. for grand larceny of a motor vehicle. Use the line called Rape, not UCR Rape*. Open Data leaves out a precinct and crime with no complaints, so count a missing one as zero.

This link runs steps 2 to 4 for September 21 to 27, 2026 and returns a CSV: count that week. Compare it with our file for that week. That week, 436 of 546 counts (78 precincts, seven crimes) matched exactly.

What we got: we checked every week from January 2015 to September 2026 for which we hold precinct numbers, 572 weeks in all. We checked each source of numbers on its own:

Citywide CompStat

Count the same seven crimes for the whole city, from every row, including the few with no precinct. This link does it for August 24 to 30, 2026. Compare it with the Citywide rows in our file for that week.

What we got: in 51 weeks of our copies, from August 2015 to August 2026, 25 of 357 counts matched exactly. Open Data held 106,482 major crimes and the reports 102,549, or 3.8% more. In 31 weeks of Josh Greenman's numbers, from March to September 2026, 14 of 217 matched, and Open Data held 3.4% more. In our 51 weeks, murder differed most: 350 in Open Data, 278 in the reports. Grand larceny of a motor vehicle was the one crime Open Data counted fewer of: 9,289, against 9,587.

Department of Correction CompStat

Count the same seven crimes, keeping only rows whose jurisdiction (jurisdiction_code) is 72, the Department of Correction. This link does it for May 25 to 31, 2026. Compare it with the Department of Correction Citywide rows in our file for that week. Both count 3 felony assaults that week.

What we got: in 41 weeks from August 2015 to May 2026, 277 of 287 counts matched exactly, and 284 were within one. Open Data held 225 major crimes and the reports 217.

NYCHA Housing Bureau report

The report counts the seven major crimes in each NYCHA development. In Open Data, the column hadevelopt names the development, but on few rows: this list shows every name in this year's data, about two dozen, where NYPD's report has 244. We counted by development, as this link does for May 25 to 31, 2026, and compared the developments whose names match exactly.

What we got: 19 names match. In 42 weeks from August 2015 to May 2026, NYPD's reports counted 623 major crimes in those 19 developments, and Open Data placed 177 there. Most single counts agree only because both are zero: of the 538 counts above zero in NYPD's reports, 133 matched. The development column cannot rebuild this report. We did not try to place complaints in developments by their map location.

The longer the period, the closer the crash counts. For a single week, Open Data held 15% to 32% more crashes than TrafficStat. For the 28 days before each report, it held 3% to 11% more, and for the year so far, 0.4% to 5.3% more. It looks as though TrafficStat's weekly number counts the crashes entered by the time the report is printed, and more arrive later. The crash dataset itself says its data is preliminary and subject to change. So for a recent week, expect Open Data to run higher than the weekly report. For a longer period, compare with NYPD's 28-day or year-to-date counts. People hurt follow the same pattern; in the year-to-date counts, Open Data ran from 1.8% lower to 3.9% higher.

By precinct. Crash rows have no precinct, so we placed each 2026 crash in the precinct whose boundary, on the Police Precincts map (version 26c), contains its latitude and longitude. Of the 74,936 crashes from January 1 to November 20, 2026, we placed 72,314 (97%). The rest have no location, or one outside every precinct. This link gives the locations for November 9 to 15, 2026. We compared 210 precinct-weeks in 2026: all 78 precincts for the week ending November 15, and a few precincts in 30 other weeks. For the year so far, 150 of the 210 crash counts were within 5% of TrafficStat's and 197 within 10%, but only 7 matched exactly. For a single week, 39 were within 5%. We did not place crashes from earlier years, because the map shows today's precinct lines, and those lines change.

Deaths are different. In all 9 weeks, Open Data counted more deaths this year than TrafficStat, from 3.8% to 20% more. From January 1 to November 15, 2026, the crash rows count 202 deaths and TrafficStat counts 178. We do not know why. The crash dataset's own description sends readers to NYPD's traffic data page and to Vision Zero View for the most accurate death counts. If you know the reason, tell us.

Traffic fatalities

Add up number_of_persons_killed in the crash rows for the 7 days, 28 days and year ending on the report's date. This link does it for January 1 to November 15, 2026. For each precinct, place the crashes as for TrafficStat.

What we got, for the week ending November 15, 2026. Our other week, ending November 22, is past the newest crash rows. Citywide, NYPD's report counts 3 deaths in 7 days, 15 in 28 days and 181 this year; Open Data counts 3, 17 and 202. By precinct, 77 of 78 counts matched for 7 days, 75 of 78 for 28 days, and 43 of 78 for the year. The precinct counts from Open Data add up to 186 for the year, not 202, because some deaths were in crashes we could not place. This report counts 181 deaths for the year where TrafficStat, for the same week, counts 178.

Quality of life

The report has 24 lines. The ones marked with an asterisk count 311 and 911 calls together, and two more count 911 calls only. The 911 dataset, NYPD Calls for Service (Year to Date), ends September 30, 2026, before the one week we hold, so we could not check those lines. We found no 311 type for “Persons in Crisis Outdoors.”

The other 12 lines count 311 requests sent to NYPD (agency is NYPD), by the day they were made (created_date) and the precinct on the request (police_precinct):

  • Total 311 Calls: every one of those requests.
  • Noise - Residential, and Noise - Vehicle: the complaint type (complaint_type) of the same name.
  • Noise - Outside: Noise - Street/Sidewalk and Noise - Park.
  • Noise - Business Related: Noise - Commercial and Noise - House of Worship.
  • Total Noise: every complaint type that starts with Noise.
  • Illegal Parking: the complaint type Illegal Parking, less the requests whose descriptor starts with Double Parked. Double Parking: those requests.
  • Blocked Driveway, Abandoned Vehicles, Drinking, and Urinating in Public: the complaint type of the same name (Abandoned Vehicle, singular).

We worked this out from the 1st Precinct's report, where all 12 lines matched, and then checked it on every precinct. Park noise and house-of-worship noise are our reading of the report: with them, Noise - Outside matched in 76 of 78 precincts instead of 38, and Noise - Business Related in 76 instead of 56. These links count October 19 to 25, 2026: requests by precinct and type, and double-parking requests.

What we got, for 78 precincts and 12 lines, 936 counts: for the week, 910 matched exactly; for 28 days, 904; for the year so far, 541. For Total 311 Calls citywide, the report counts 34,329 for the week and 1,407,672 for the year; Open Data counts 34,126 and 1,396,197, under 1% fewer.

How we use AI #

BetaNYC uses AI tools openly and with human accountability. The code that saves and reads NYPD's files, and the pages of this site, were written with help from AI coding assistants, under the direction and review of BetaNYC staff. Automated tests check the code on every change.

The numbers are not written by AI. A program copies them from NYPD's own spreadsheets, cell by cell, and stops if a file's layout changes rather than guess. NYPD's original files are kept unchanged in the releases, so you can check any number against the file it came from.

Read BetaNYC's AI policy. Questions about our approach: lab@beta.nyc.

Contributing #

Found a wrong number, a missing week, or an NYPD file we don't save? Open an issue on GitHub. Pull requests are welcome.

Every change to the published data, or to where the files are kept, is listed on What's changed, and so is every correction when we get something wrong.

If you saved any of NYPD's weekly files yourself, from any week before November 2026, we would like to hear from you. Each one can fill a gap in the charts.
